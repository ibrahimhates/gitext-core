# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Judge code, not the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- **Assume competence and good faith.** Someone who did something differently usually had a
  reason. Ask what it was before concluding they were careless.
- **Critique the work, not the author.** "This breaks on detached HEAD" is useful. "You clearly
  didn't test this" is not — it adds nothing to the first sentence except an accusation.
- **Accept that you may be wrong.** This project's own history is full of confidently held
  assumptions that measurement destroyed. That happens to everyone here.
- **Be patient with beginners.** Everyone using this project already knows Git; not everyone
  knows .NET, Avalonia, or this codebase.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Demeaning comments about anyone's identity — including but not limited to age, disability,
  ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion,
  or sexual orientation.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or persistently reopening decisions that have been made
  and documented, without new evidence.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages —
and whenever someone is representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report problems to the maintainer at **atesibrahim612@gmail.com**, or by opening an issue if
the matter is not sensitive. Reports are handled privately, and the reporter's identity is not
disclosed.

The maintainer may remove comments, close issues or pull requests, or block accounts, in
proportion to what happened. For a first, minor incident the response will usually be a private
note explaining the problem. Repeated or serious behaviour results in a ban.

This project is small enough that there is no committee: the maintainer decides, and is also
bound by this document.
